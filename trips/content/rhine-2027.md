## Day by day

The full route is about 275 km, so the riding days are long but flat. Strong riders and teenagers ride every kilometre. Younger children take a train or boat for the dullest flat stretch each day and still ride past all the castles.

| Day | Ride | Full ride | Family option |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fri 28 May | Arrive in Frankfurt | – | Evening flights, bikes already waiting at the hotel. Quick briefing, early night. |
| Sat 29 May | Frankfurt – Mainz – Rüdesheim – St Goar | ~100 km, flat | S-Bahn Frankfurt–Mainz with bikes, then ride ~60 km through the vineyards and into the Rhine Gorge |
| Sun 30 May | St Goar – Loreley – Koblenz – Bonn | ~100 km, flat | KD boat St Goar–Koblenz past the castles, then ride ~65 km to Bonn |
| Mon 31 May | Rest day in Bonn | – | Rhine promenade, Beethoven's birthplace, or a 20-minute train to Cologne cathedral |
| Tue 1 June | Bonn – Cologne – Düsseldorf | ~75 km, flat | Train Bonn–Cologne, then ride ~45 km. Everyone finishes together on Düsseldorf's riverside promenade. |
| Wed 2 June | Home | – | Bikes into the lorry, fly home from Düsseldorf |

The days follow the Rhine cycle route (EuroVelo 15) and the Main path, mostly on traffic-free riverside tracks. Regional trains run alongside the river the whole way, so anyone can stop at any station.

## Riding with children

The youngest riders set the pace for their group, and every day has a way to make it shorter.

- **Ages:** 10 and over. Under-18s ride with a parent or an adult the parents have named in writing, as the Framework requires.
- **Groups:** a dedicated family group or two, each with a leader and a back marker. Adults without children ride in their own groups and wait at the café with the cake.
- **Shortcuts:** the KD Rhine boats between Bingen and Koblenz take bikes when there's room, for about €3.50 per bike per trip. Regional trains run alongside the river all the way to Düsseldorf.
- **Sweep van:** the support van follows the family groups and can carry a tired rider and bike to the next hotel.
- **Kit for children:** a helmet, a bike that has been serviced, a lock, a rain jacket, and the snacks that stop meltdowns.
- **Consent:** every under-18 needs a parental consent and medical form before the trip. We'll send it to you.
- **Rest day in Bonn:** the Rhine promenade, the Haribo shop and a quick train to Cologne cathedral, so nobody needs to sit in a museum.

## Getting there and back

You book your own flights: into Frankfurt on Friday evening 28 May, and home from Düsseldorf on Wednesday 2 June. The club books the hotels. Half-term fares go up quickly, so book early once your place is confirmed.

Your bike travels separately. The bike lorry and its driver leave on Thursday 27 May so the bikes are waiting in Frankfurt, then shadow the route as a second support vehicle and bring them home.

## Before you go

- **Passports:** everyone needs their own, including children. A child travelling with an adult who isn't their parent needs a signed consent letter from the parents.
- **Health cover:** a GHIC or EHIC card plus travel insurance that covers cycling, for adults and children alike.
- **Rhine levels:** spring high water can close sections of the riverside path. We'll check the river forecast the week before and plan diversions.
- **Sunday shopping:** German shops close on Sundays, so stock up on Saturday evening in St Goar. Cafés and bakeries in the wine villages usually open.
- **Path manners:** the Rhine path is busy with families and e-bikes in early June. Use your bell, keep right and ride single file through towns.

## How payment works

Like every W/Rhinos trip, this one runs at cost. You pay a deposit when you register, an interim payment if a hotel needs money before we go, and the final balance after the trip once every cost is known. A flat £50 per rider goes to a charity the riders choose. Details are in the Framework.
